- Poland's democratic repair since 2023 is partial: what the government controls (media, prosecution practice, judges' discipline, civil society) came back, while what needs the President or the Constitutional Court's s approval is still compromised (legal dualism).  

- Post illiberal dilemma: the new government has to choose, to restore democracy, only two paths between effectiveness, swiftness and legality. It often leaves out the third principle, leading to decisionist practices that spur malcontent across the legalists and the opponents, which call for assault on democracy (decisionist majoritarianism).  

- 2023 camp lost ground: Tusk's party (Civic Coalition) increased its support, but the other parties within the coalition saw their support decline. PiS fragmented and fell in polls, while other far-right parties emerged.  

- While young people are growing more and more secular and less catholic, they are growing more extremist on the right - far right parties (Konfederazja) are surging among young people.  

TRENDS PROPOSALS:  

1) How did the role of women impact the revival of democracy in Poland, and - since we know it was consequential to the final, liberal outcome - how do they impact politics in other European countries? How can we predict, based on feminist activity in different countries, pro-democracy push?  

2) We know that - due to PiS’ capture over the legal bodies of status quo change, such as the Constitutional Court and the Presidency, which holds strong veto power - Tusk’s government is currently utilising borderline methods to erase anti-democratic measures previously taken by PiS, with the aim of restoring democracy. Therefore, how can we assess whether these borderline, decisionist measures can be justified (method justifies scope?), after illiberal capture by an incumbent, non-democratic reality? What is the tradeoff between legalism, efficacy and speed, and how can we predict its usage in Hungary (which is now exiting an illiberal order), or looking at similar past cases in Slovakia and Czechia in 2021? 

3) Even though Poland had a clear chance to completely eradicate its previous anti-eu, anti-democracy stance, that eradication only happened partly with the 2023 parliamentary elections. Therefore 2025 presidential elections in fact showed a strong, right-wing signal from the population: PiS’ candidate got elected. Moreover, nowadays, along a fragmentation of the old PiS party, new extremist parties are rising. These parties, unlike the old traditional PiS way, are growing less catholic but more extreme. How can we explain this ongoing relationship in the hard right between secularism and extremism, and how is it reflected in other countries, such as Germany’s AFD?
