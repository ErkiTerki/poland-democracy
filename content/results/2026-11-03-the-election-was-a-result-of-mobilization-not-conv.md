According to the Ipsos exit poll (info from The Polish Voter), 87% of 2019 PiS voters stayed with PiS. Of 2019 non-voters, 64% chose the opposition (KO 31%, Third Way 17.9%, Left 15.2%) and 15.5% chose PiS.
- Cześnik et al.: how someone voted in 2019 is by far the strongest predictor of their 2023 vote. Voters were loyal, so turnout determined the outcome.

Polish National Election Study: negative party identification rose from 39% to 76% over four years, with divisive issues more prominent in 2023. 

Tusk explicitly urged undecided voters to back the opposition coalition despite any doubts.

Young voter turnout rose from 46.4% to 68.8%, PiS share fell form 26.3% to 14.9%. KO led their bote, Konfederacja second (around 20%).

Women's turnout rose from 61.5% to 73.7%. Cześnik et al. find no gender gap in the PiS or KO vote (36.1% PiS among both men and women). 

PiS core: Older, less educated, rural voters, farmer, pensioners, manual workers. 63% of voters with only primary education chose PiS

Electoral map still follows 18th-19th century partitions of Poland: PiS is strongest in the east (the former Russian zone) and south-east (former Austrian), the opposition in the centre-west (former Prussian) and the western territories that became Polish after WWII.

2023 strategy centered on opposition coordinating without merging. Three separate lists aimed at different voters (liberal urban, conservative, and rural left).

Senate Pact: KO, the Left, PSL and Poland 2050 signed it on 28 February 2023, backing one shared candidate in 97 of 100 districts, and won 66 seats (up from 51 in 2019). They also cooperated closely on campaigning, with only one candidate per district allowed to use the coalition logo.
