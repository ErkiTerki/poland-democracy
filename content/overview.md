title: Poland's democratic U-turn
---
**Challenge:** *Democracy, Political Trust and Radical Voting in Europe and Beyond* — Open Data University (Latitudes), HEC DSO Capstone 2026–27.

**Working question (draft):** Poland's liberal democracy score fell sharply under PiS (2015–2023) and has recovered since the October 2023 election. **Which parts of democracy came back, which didn't, and do the different democracy indices agree on it?**

**What we know so far**
- V-Dem liberal democracy index (v16, 2026 release): 0.827 in 2010 → 0.417 in 2022 → 0.645 in 2025. Hungary kept falling over the same period (0.315 in 2025).
- The recovery is uneven. By 2025, freedom of expression (0.917) and legislative constraints on the executive (0.917) are back near pre-2015 levels, but judicial constraints are still 0.714 (vs ~0.96 before 2015). See **Charts**.
- That matches the politics. In Feb 2026, President Nawrocki vetoed the bill reforming the National Council of the Judiciary (KRS), and the coalition lacks the 3/5 majority to override. The next parliamentary election is in 2027.
- V-Dem's 2024 report called the first (2023) uptick "small but statistically insignificant". **Uncertainty intervals matter here.** We plot central estimates only so far.
- The October 2023 election had record turnout (~74%).

**Rules from the brief we must follow**
1. For every index: say what it counts as democracy, who judged it, and how wide the interval is.
2. For every "radical/populist" party label: name the source and version (PopuList vs CHES), give the definition in plain language, and ship the classification table.
3. Keep description, prediction and causal claims separate. Country-level correlations say nothing about how individuals voted.
4. We need a project owner or audience. Candidates: Batory Foundation, Helsinki Foundation for Human Rights, OKO.press, Notes from Poland.
