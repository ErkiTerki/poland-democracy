1. POST-1989: ECONOMIC COLLAPSE AND POLITICAL INSTABILITY 
• Economic collapse: inflation spiked to 585% in 1990. Only in 1999 did it fall below 10% for the first time, reaching 7%.
• Political instability: eight administrations in eight years.
• Late 1990s: Jerzy Buzek, from the centre-right AWS, became the first prime minister to serve four years in office.

2. THE RESHAPING OF THE PARTY SYSTEM 
• After Buzek’s term, the two mainstream parties originated from the ashes of AWS: Civic Platform (PO), centre-right, and Law and Justice (PiS), further right.
• After 2004, the left-wing party, which had held a considerable share of seats in the Sejm, collapsed following a corruption scandal. Since then, the left has almost entirely disappeared from Polish politics.
• Therefore, what used to be the centre-right wing of parliament became the parliament: Civic Platform became the new “left”, while PiS became the new mainstream right.

3. THE KACZYŃSKI BROTHERS’ FIRST PERIOD IN POWER, 2005–2007 
Rise to power
• In 2005, the Kaczyński brothers rose to power on an anti-corruption platform.
• Lech Kaczyński, previously mayor of Warsaw, ran against Donald Tusk. After winning the presidency, he picked Kazimierz Marcinkiewicz as prime minister.
• Marcinkiewicz, despite being one of Poland’s most popular and moderate politicians, resigned shortly afterwards. Jarosław Kaczyński took over.
• The twins were therefore managing the government: Lech as president and Jarosław as prime minister.
The “Moral Revolution”
• They called for a “Moral Revolution”, centred on a return to Polish identity.
• They created an anti-corruption bureau and pursued a “purification” of Poland: an anti-communist witch hunt that banned everyone involved with the previous communist regime from participating in politics.
• This law was also used to fire journalists and replace them with their own. 
Government collapse
• Tensions with Germany increased in 2007.
• Jarosław’s government then collapsed, leading to new elections, which he lost.

4. TUSK’S GOVERNMENT AND THE SMOLENSK CRASH 
Tusk’s policies and declining popularity
• Tusk subsequently came to power.
• Instead of implementing his promised liberal policies, he increased VAT and taxes on coal, oil and tobacco.
• His administration also faced allegations of collusion.
• Raising the retirement age particularly hurt him in the polls. 
The 2010 plane crash
• Lech Kaczyński died in a plane crash in 2010.
• Major conspiracy theories developed around his death. His twin brother, Jarosław, suspected Putin’s involvement.
• In 2015, on the fifth anniversary of the crash, PiS held massive rallies, which helped the party return to power.

5. IMMIGRATION AND CHANGING PUBLIC ATTITUDES 
Emigration and earlier attitudes
• In the early 2000s, thousands of Poles emigrated to other European countries thanks to freedom of movement.
• In the World Values Survey, in 2010–2014, only 7.2% of Poles did not want immigrants as neighbours.
• In 2017–2022, that figure rose to approximately 19%, alongside increasing backlash. 
The two migration crises of 2015
• Two migration crises emerged: migration from Ukraine following the conflict over Crimea, and migration from Syria and Africa.
• Civic Platform’s government decided to open Poland’s borders to some of these migrants, contributing to a major decrease in its popularity.
• This marked a major shift in Poland’s immigration policy. 
The 2020 European Court of Justice ruling
• In 2020, the European Court of Justice ruled that Hungary, Poland and Czechia had broken the law by failing to accept refugees and asylum seekers. 
The Belarus border crisis, 2021
• Lukashenko deliberately sent thousands of refugees to the Polish border to spark conflict within the EU.
• Poland responded with strong opposition, framing the situation as hybrid war. 
Ukrainian refugees and different treatment, 2022–2023
• In 2022, Ukrainian refugees were warmly welcomed by Poles.
• Meanwhile, Arab refugees were depicted as rapists and people seeking to destabilise Poland.
• In 2022–2023, PiS politicians increasingly referred to them as “illegal immigrants”, rather than directly attributing their opposition to Islam. In 2015, religious opposition had been more explicit. 
Broader discourse on immigration
• A common discourse on Polish social media is that Western Europe has experienced declining social and ethnic homogeneity, alongside an erosion of safety, because of immigration policies.
• Poland is therefore oriented towards maintaining a very safe country with a high degree of ethnic homogeneity.
• More recently, attitudes towards Ukrainian refugees have also changed, alongside general hostility towards cultural difference.
• A common view is that Poland, unlike Western countries, never had a colonial empire and therefore should not share the same burden.
• There is also a feeling that Poland lacks the resources to support everyone. 
Comparison with Hungary
• PiS in Poland and Fidesz in Hungary were broadly aligned, apart from their stance on Russia and the intensity of their anti-immigration stance.
• On Russia, Poland was opposed, while Hungary was friendly.

6. PiS’S RETURN TO POWER: WELFARE AND INSTITUTIONAL CONTROL 
Political appeal and benefits
• PiS knew how to channel people’s anger.
• Once in power, it introduced child benefits and retirement benefits to increase support and provide immediate gratification. 
Control over the courts
• Before these measures, one of PiS’s first actions was to take control of the courts.
• In 2015, it forced the appointment of five constitutional judges, causing a constitutional crisis.
• In 2019, it passed a law allowing the dismissal of any judge who “engages in political activity”—a phrase open to ambiguous interpretation.
• The EU blocked funding to both Poland and Hungary because of their democratic backsliding. 
Abortion and protests
• In 2020, the Polish government used its control of the courts to ban abortion, sparking massive protests.
• This was probably the moment when many people began to realise that democracy was being taken away from them.
• The attack on judicial independence also mobilised people, but it did not gain the same traction as the attack on women’s rights. 
LGBT-free zones and public television
• LGBT-free zones, 2020: a campaign of state-backed hate speech involving municipalities accounting for one-third of the country.
• The government also strengthened its control over public television, ultimately bringing it completely into PiS’s hands.

7. THE 2023 ELECTION CAMPAIGN 
Visagate
• The scandal emerged in the run-up to the 2023 election.
• It showed that the government had illegally issued up to 350,000 work visas to people from Asia and the Middle East in exchange for bribes.
• Breaking just before the election, the scandal destroyed support among anti-immigration voters. 
Main electoral blocs
• PiS: Law and Justice.
• Civic Coalition: the bloc associated with Donald Tusk.
• Third Way: an alternative for moderately conservative Christians who felt PiS was too far right.
• The Left: progressive social democrats.
• Confederation: far right, combining fundamentalist Christian views with radical libertarian policies. Its support was mostly composed of young men. 
Confederation: positions and leadership
• Economic proposals included removing income tax, reducing corporate taxes and eliminating socialised healthcare.
• Other positions included a stricter abortion ban and legalising firearms.
• Pro-Russian, implicitly, and explicitly opposed to Ukraine.
• One of its founders believed women should not have the right to vote, although he was no longer in the leadership.
• In 2023, Sławomir Mentzen was a leader. According to him, Poland did not want “Jews, homosexuals, abortion, taxation and the EU”.

7. THE OCTOBER 15, 2023 ELECTION AND TUSK’S RETURN 
Turnout and results
• An exceptionally high 74% turnout, with a major surge among young voters and especially women.
• Victory for the centre-right.
• PiS lost seats, while Confederation substantially underperformed compared with expectations.
• The election paved the way for Donald Tusk’s return to power. 
Tusk’s new direction
• Obtaining EU funding.
• Making television public again.
• Legalising abortion again.
• Restoring judicial independence.
• Maintaining the existing stance on immigration. 
Obstacles to reform
• PiS had packed the courts with PiS judges.
• The presidency also remained in PiS’s hands: Andrzej Duda from 2015 to 2025, and Karol Nawrocki from 2025.
• Both presidents have sought to slow the process of re-democratisation following Tusk’s return.

8. REFLECTIONS ON POLAND’S DEMOCRATIC RESILIENCE 
• Strong attempts to undermine Polish democracy ultimately did not fully succeed, as reflected in the election turnout.
• Protests defending women’s rights, the constitution and judicial independence translated into high anti-PiS turnout.
